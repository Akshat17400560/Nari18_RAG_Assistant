# Nari18 Choosing Stitched Suits

## Overview

Choosing a stitched suit is mainly about finding the right combination of **fabric, occasion, fit, silhouette, neckline, sleeves, colour, and finishing**.

For Nari18, customers primarily select an unstitched suit set and can use the brand's custom-stitching service to convert the fabric into a finished garment. Nari18 offers custom stitching on many pieces and provides guidance for measurements, lining, finishing, neckline, sleeve style, and overall fit.

A good stitched-suit choice should therefore consider both the **original fabric/design** and the **final garment the customer wants to create**.

## 1. Start With the Occasion

The first question should be:

**Where will the stitched suit be worn?**

The occasion helps determine the appropriate fabric, silhouette, embellishment, colour, and overall level of formality.

| Occasion | Suitable Fabric Direction | Suggested Style |
| Wedding | Silk, Chanderi, Tissue | Elegant, festive, rich |
| Wedding guest | Chanderi, Silk, Georgette | Refined and comfortable |
| Festive celebration | Chanderi, Georgette, Silk | Traditional and dressy |
| Evening event | Organza, Georgette, Crepe | Statement and flowing |
| Office wear | Linen, Cotton | Structured and polished |
| Daily wear | Cotton, Muslin | Comfortable and practical |
| Summer event | Cotton, Mul-Cotton, Muslin, Linen | Lightweight and breathable |
| Mehendi/Haldi | Chiffon, Georgette | Light, colourful and flowing |

These are general fabric recommendations. The exact suitability of a suit depends on the individual product's construction, embroidery, colour, and fabric details.

## 2. Choose the Fabric First

The fabric strongly influences how the finished stitched suit will look and feel.

### Silk

Silk is suitable for customers seeking a luxurious and formal appearance.

Consider Silk for:

- Weddings
- Sangeet
- Diwali
- Grand family celebrations
- Formal festive occasions

Silk works particularly well with straight or A-line silhouettes where its drape and richness can remain visible.

### Chanderi

Chanderi is a versatile choice for festive and wedding-guest outfits.

Consider Chanderi for:

- Daytime weddings
- Roka ceremonies
- Mehendi
- Festive lunches
- Elegant traditional wear

It can provide a festive appearance without the heavier feel associated with some richer fabrics.

### Organza

Organza has a crisp structure and can create a more statement-oriented finished garment.

Consider Organza for:

- Evening events
- Receptions
- Formal dinners
- Party wear
- Structured silhouettes

The construction should be planned carefully because the fabric's structure can influence the final fit and fall.

### Georgette

Georgette is useful when a customer wants movement and a softer, flowing appearance.

Consider Georgette for:

- Receptions
- Festive gatherings
- Evening dinners
- Embroidered suits
- Flowing silhouettes

Nari18 also features Georgette suits with Chikankari and other decorative work.

### Cotton

Cotton is a practical choice for regular use.

Consider Cotton for:

- Office wear
- Everyday ethnic wear
- Casual outings
- Summer dressing
- Family gatherings

### Muslin and Mul-Cotton

These fabrics are suitable when lightweight comfort is a priority.

Consider them for:

- Summer
- Daily wear
- Casual events
- Warm-weather office wear
- Lightweight silhouettes

### Linen

Linen is particularly useful for polished everyday and professional styling.

Consider Linen for:

- Office wear
- Business meetings
- Smart casual events
- Travel
- Summer workwear

### Tissue

Tissue provides a more festive and luxurious appearance.

Consider Tissue for:

- Weddings
- Religious ceremonies
- Festive celebrations
- Evening functions
- Special occasions

### Chiffon

Chiffon can create a lightweight and flowing finished garment.

Consider Chiffon for:

- Mehendi
- Haldi
- Festive lunches
- Summer celebrations
- Light evening styling

### Crepe

Crepe provides a smooth, elegant fall and is suitable for more polished evening looks.

Consider Crepe for:

- Parties
- Dinners
- Evening events
- Festive nights

## 3. Choose the Right Silhouette

Once the fabric and occasion are selected, decide how the finished suit should be shaped.

Nari18's custom-stitching guidance describes several common silhouettes.

### Straight-Cut Suit

A straight-cut kurta is a versatile option for both everyday and formal wear.

It can be paired with:

- Straight pants
- Salwar
- Churidar
- Palazzo

This is a practical option when the customer wants a clean and relatively timeless appearance.

### A-Line Suit

An A-line kurta gradually widens toward the hem.

It can work well for:

- Festive wear
- Wedding guests
- Elegant daytime outfits
- Comfortable movement

### Anarkali-Style Suit

A flared Anarkali-style kurta creates a more dramatic and traditional appearance.

It can be suitable for:

- Weddings
- Festive celebrations
- Formal ethnic events
- Special occasions

The amount of flare should be considered before cutting the fabric because it affects fabric consumption.

### Short Kurta With Sharara

A shorter kurta paired with a sharara creates a more contemporary festive appearance.

It can be suitable for:

- Mehendi
- Sangeet
- Festive parties
- Celebrations

### Short Kurta With Wide-Leg Pants

This combination provides a modern ethnic appearance and can work particularly well with lightweight fabrics.

## 4. Select the Neckline

The neckline has a significant effect on the appearance of the finished kurta.

Nari18's guides discuss neckline options such as:

- Round neck
- V-neck
- Boat neck
- Keyhole
- Mandarin collar

When selecting a neckline, consider:

- Personal comfort
- Desired level of coverage
- Fabric
- Embroidery placement
- Occasion
- Existing design of the fabric

For heavily embroidered fabrics, the neckline should be planned so that important embroidery or borders are not unnecessarily removed during cutting.

## 5. Select the Sleeve Style

Common sleeve preferences include:

- Full sleeves
- Three-quarter sleeves
- Half sleeves
- Sleeveless

Sleeve selection should consider the occasion and fabric.

For example:

- Full sleeves can provide a more formal appearance.
- Three-quarter sleeves are versatile for office and festive wear.
- Half sleeves can be comfortable for warm weather.
- Sleeveless designs can work for selected contemporary or evening styles.

The original embroidery and border placement should also be considered before finalizing sleeve length.

## 6. Get the Measurements Right

For a custom-stitched Nari18 suit, accurate measurements are one of the most important parts of the process.

Useful measurements include:

- Bust
- Waist
- Hip
- Shoulder width
- Kurta length
- Sleeve length
- Bottom inseam

Measurements should ideally be taken while wearing the undergarment normally worn with the suit. Measurements should not be taken over bulky clothing.

The quality of the final fit depends heavily on the accuracy and consistency of the measurements provided.

## 7. Decide the Kurta Length

Kurta length should be selected before stitching.

Possible preferences include:

- Short kurta
- Knee-length kurta
- Calf-length kurta
- Longer traditional kurta

The appropriate length depends on:

- Height
- Body proportions
- Silhouette
- Bottom style
- Occasion
- Personal preference

For example, a short kurta may work well with sharara or wide-leg pants, while a longer straight kurta can work with straight pants or churidar.

## 8. Choose the Bottom Style

The bottom should complement the kurta rather than being selected independently.

Possible options include:

- Straight pants
- Salwar
- Churidar
- Palazzo
- Sharara
- Wide-leg pants

Consider the relationship between the upper garment and bottom.

Examples:

- Straight kurta + straight pants = clean and classic
- A-line kurta + churidar = traditional and fitted
- Flared Anarkali + palazzo = flowing festive look
- Short kurta + sharara = contemporary festive styling
- Short kurta + wide-leg pants = modern ethnic look

## 9. Consider the Existing Fabric Design

One of the most important considerations when converting an unstitched suit into a stitched garment is **where the existing design will appear after cutting**.

Before stitching, examine:

- Embroidery placement
- Printed motifs
- Borders
- Zari work
- Gotta-Patti
- Chikankari
- Decorative panels
- Dupatta design
- Contrast elements

The tailor needs to position these elements correctly so that important design details remain visible on the finished garment.

For example, a decorative border may be intended for the hem or sleeve, while a central embroidered motif may be intended for the front panel.

## 10. Think About the Dupatta

The dupatta is an important part of the completed suit.

When selecting the stitched outfit, check:

- Dupatta colour
- Fabric
- Print or embroidery
- Border
- Tassels
- Contrast elements
- Overall coordination with the stitched kurta and bottom

The kurta does not have to exactly match the dupatta in every detail. Contrast styling can also be used where the original Nari18 product is designed that way.

## 12. Match the Fabric to the Climate

Indian weather should be considered when choosing a stitched suit.

For hot weather, Nari18's guidance recommends lightweight fabrics such as:

- Cotton
- Mul-Cotton
- Muslin
- Linen

Chanderi can also be considered when the customer wants a more festive look while remaining relatively lightweight.

For cooler weather or grander occasions, fabrics such as Silk and Tissue can be considered depending on the desired appearance.

## 13. Consider Embroidery and Handwork

Embellishment can change both the appearance and the appropriate use of a stitched suit.

Nari18's products and guides reference techniques and details such as:

- Chikankari
- Hand embroidery
- Zari
- Kantha work
- Gotta-Patti

- Decorative borders

When selecting a heavily worked suit, consider whether the embroidery is appropriate for the occasion and whether the desired silhouette will preserve the important design elements.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Choosing the Fabric Only by Appearance

A fabric may look beautiful in photographs but may not be appropriate for the intended weather or occasion.

### Ignoring Fabric Behaviour

Silk, Organza, Chanderi, Cotton, Linen, and Georgette behave differently during cutting and stitching. The final silhouette should be compatible with the fabric.

### Providing Inaccurate Measurements

Incorrect measurements can affect the final fit. Measurements should be taken carefully and consistently.

### Ignoring Embroidery Placement

A beautiful border or central motif can lose its impact if it is not considered during cutting.

### Selecting a Silhouette Too Late

The silhouette affects fabric usage and cutting. It should be decided before tailoring begins.

### Assuming All Products Have the Same Stitching Options

Nari18 custom stitching is available on many products, but exact availability should be confirmed for the selected product.

### Ignoring Exchange Conditions

Custom-stitched garments are made according to customer-specific requirements. Customers should understand the applicable policy before placing the order.
