# Nari18 Fabric Comparison

## Overview

Nari18 offers unstitched suits across a range of fabrics, each with different characteristics in terms of comfort, appearance, drape, structure, occasion suitability, seasonality and care requirements.

The best fabric depends on what the customer needs from the outfit. A lightweight cotton or Muslin suit may be better for everyday summer wear, while Silk, Tissue or Organza may be more appropriate for weddings and formal celebrations.

Nari18's current catalog includes fabric categories such as Silk, Organza, Tissue, Muslin, Cotton, Linen, Georgette, Chanderi, Crepe, Chiffon, Lycra Net and Mul-Cotton. The availability and number of products in each category can change over time.

## Silk vs Chanderi

### Silk

Silk is the stronger choice when the customer wants a rich, luxurious and formal appearance. It has natural sheen and works particularly well for grand celebrations and wedding-related occasions.

**Advantages:**
- Rich appearance
- Elegant natural sheen
- Excellent for festive styling
- Suitable for embroidery and detailed work
- Strong choice for weddings and formal occasions

**Considerations:**
- Usually requires more careful maintenance
- Can feel warmer than lightweight summer fabrics
- Less suitable for very hot everyday wear

### Chanderi

Chanderi provides a lighter alternative for customers who want festive elegance without the heavier feel associated with some silk garments.

**Advantages:**
- Lightweight
- Traditional appearance
- Suitable for festive occasions
- Good option for warm-weather celebrations
- Works well with zari and other decorative details

**Considerations:**
- Requires more delicate care than everyday cotton
- May need appropriate lining depending on sheerness
- Exact characteristics vary by product

**Choose Silk when:** the priority is richness and formal presence.

**Choose Chanderi when:** the priority is lightweight festive elegance.

Nari18's guides position Silk strongly for weddings and major festive events, while Chanderi is presented as a lightweight festive and wedding-guest option.

## Silk vs Organza

Both Silk and Organza can create premium occasion wear, but their visual character is different.

| Feature | Silk | Organza |
| Appearance | Rich and fluid | Crisp and structured |
| Drape | Fluid | Holds shape |
| Feel | Smooth and luxurious | Lightweight but structured |
| Best for | Weddings, festive wear | Parties, receptions, evening events |
| Style | Traditional luxury | Statement and contemporary |
| Structure | Medium | High |

Silk is generally better when the customer wants a classic luxurious appearance, while Organza is better when the customer wants a structured, statement-making silhouette.

Nari18 describes Organza as particularly suitable for evening and party wear because of its crisp body and ability to hold shape.

## Cotton vs Mul-Cotton vs Muslin

These three are among the strongest options for customers prioritizing comfort and warm-weather wear.

### Cotton

Cotton is versatile and practical. It is suitable for everyday wear, office wear, casual outings and summer use.

### Mul-Cotton

Mul-Cotton is especially lightweight and soft. It is a strong option when the customer wants maximum comfort during hot weather.

### Muslin

Muslin is soft, airy and fluid. It provides a slightly more delicate feel than conventional everyday cotton.

| Feature | Cotton | Mul-Cotton | Muslin |
| Comfort | Very high | Very high | Very high |
| Breathability | Very high | Very high | Very high |
| Feel | Natural | Very soft | Butter-soft |
| Structure | Medium | Low-medium | Low |
| Summer suitability | Excellent | Excellent | Excellent |
| Daily wear | Excellent | Excellent | Excellent |
| Office wear | Excellent | Good | Good |
| Festive use | Depends on design | Depends on design | Depends on design |

Nari18's summer guide identifies cotton, particularly Mulmul/Mul-Cotton, as one of the strongest summer choices and describes Muslin as another highly comfortable option.

## Cotton vs Linen

Cotton and Linen are both strong choices for summer, but they create different visual impressions.

### Cotton

Choose Cotton when comfort, softness and everyday practicality are the priority.

### Linen

Choose Linen when the customer wants a more polished and structured appearance while retaining good breathability.

| Feature | Cotton | Linen |
| Comfort | Very high | High |
| Breathability | Very high | Very high |
| Appearance | Casual to versatile | Polished and refined |
| Structure | Medium | Medium-high |
| Office suitability | Excellent | Excellent |
| Casual suitability | Excellent | Excellent |
| Summer suitability | Excellent | Excellent |
| Maintenance | Generally easier | Requires more attention to creasing |

Nari18's summer guidance positions Cotton as a summer essential and Linen as a sophisticated, breathable option particularly suited to office and formal daytime wear.

## Chanderi vs Georgette

Both can work well for festive occasions, but they produce different silhouettes.

### Chanderi

Chanderi is better for customers seeking a traditional, lightweight and slightly structured festive appearance.

### Georgette

Georgette is better when the customer wants fluid movement, softer drape and a more flowing silhouette.

| Feature | Chanderi | Georgette |
| Weight | Lightweight | Lightweight |
| Drape | Elegant, moderate structure | Fluid |
| Look | Traditional | Flowing and feminine |
| Festive suitability | Excellent | Excellent |
| Wedding guest suitability | Excellent | Excellent |
| Evening suitability | Good | Excellent |
| Chikankari suitability | Product dependent | Strong |
| Structure | Medium | Low-medium |

Nari18's guides associate Chanderi with festive and wedding-guest wear and Georgette with festive evenings, receptions and detailed embroidery such as Chikankari.

## Organza vs Georgette

These fabrics are particularly useful when customers are deciding between a structured and flowing look.

**Organza:**
- Crisp
- Structured
- Holds shape
- Statement-making
- Strong for parties and receptions

**Georgette:**
- Fluid
- Softly draped
- Movement-oriented
- Suitable for embroidery
- Strong for festive and evening wear

**Choose Organza for:** a structured, elegant and statement silhouette.

**Choose Georgette for:** a softer, flowing and graceful silhouette.

## Tissue vs Silk

Tissue and Silk are both premium-looking fabrics but have different visual effects.

| Feature | Silk | Tissue |
| Surface appearance | Natural sheen | Noticeable shimmer |
| Feel | Smooth | Structured/light |
| Look | Rich and luxurious | Festive and luminous |
| Best occasion | Weddings, festive events | Ceremonies, festive celebrations |
| Silhouette | Fluid to structured | More structured |
| Visual impact | Elegant | High-impact |

Tissue is particularly useful when the customer wants a shimmering festive appearance, while Silk is more appropriate when the desired effect is classic luxury.

Nari18's guide specifically describes Tissue as having a metallic/festive sheen and recommends it for grand celebrations.

## Chiffon vs Georgette

Both Chiffon and Georgette can provide lightweight, flowing outfits.

### Chiffon

Chiffon is particularly useful for very light, fluid and soft silhouettes. Nari18's fabric guide associates Chiffon with evening and party wear and also recommends it for functions such as Mehendi and Haldi when used in suitable colours.

### Georgette

Georgette has a more textured surface and is particularly strong for festive looks and embroidered designs.

**Choose Chiffon for:** lightweight, soft and flowing styling.

**Choose Georgette for:** a slightly more textured, festive and embroidery-friendly appearance.

## Linen vs Muslin

These fabrics are both useful for summer and everyday ethnic wear but serve different styling preferences.

**Linen:**
- More structured
- More polished
- Strong for office wear
- Naturally textured
- Can crease easily

**Muslin:**
- Softer
- More fluid
- Lightweight
- Comfortable for daily wear
- Particularly suitable for warm weather

Nari18's guides position Linen toward polished office and daytime wear, while Muslin is emphasized for comfortable everyday and warm-weather use.

## Fabric Comparison for Custom Stitching

Nari18's unstitched-suit model allows many products to be converted into finished garments through custom stitching.

Fabric choice should therefore be considered together with the intended silhouette.

Examples:

- **Silk:** works well with elegant straight or A-line silhouettes.
- **Chanderi:** suitable for traditional straight or flared silhouettes.
- **Organza:** benefits from silhouettes that take advantage of its structure.
- **Georgette:** works well with flowing silhouettes.
- **Cotton:** versatile for straight, A-line and everyday silhouettes.
- **Muslin:** suitable for relaxed and fluid designs.
- **Linen:** works particularly well with clean, structured silhouettes.

The exact stitching options depend on the individual Nari18 product and should not be assumed solely from the fabric category.

## Simple Fabric Selection Guide

If a customer says:

**"I want something for daily wear."**

→ Start with Cotton, Mul-Cotton or Muslin.

**"I need something for office wear."**

→ Start with Linen, Cotton or Muslin.

**"I need something comfortable for summer."**

→ Start with Mul-Cotton, Cotton or Muslin; Linen is another strong option.

**"I am attending a wedding."**

→ Consider Silk, Chanderi, Organza, Tissue or Georgette depending on the formality and time of the event.

**"I want something elegant but lightweight."**

→ Consider Chanderi or Georgette.

**"I want a structured designer look."**

→ Consider Organza or Tissue.

**"I want a flowing outfit."**

→ Consider Georgette or Chiffon.

**"I want a luxurious traditional look."**

→ Consider Silk or Tissue.

## RAG Usage Notes

When using this comparison document to answer customer questions:

1. Treat fabric characteristics as general guidance, not absolute rules.
2. Do not claim that every product made from a particular fabric has identical weight, transparency, drape or composition.
3. Always verify the individual product page for exact fabric composition when available.
4. Do not infer that all Silk products are pure silk unless the product description explicitly states it.
5. Do not infer a blend, such as silk-cotton, unless the product information confirms it.
6. Do not assume every Organza, Chanderi, Tissue or Georgette product has identical structure or transparency.
7. Do not assume all cotton products are identical; Cotton, Mul-Cotton and other cotton-based fabrics can feel and behave differently.
8. Do not guarantee that a particular fabric will be comfortable for every person or climate.
9. Do not provide current prices from this comparison file because prices and product availability change.
10. Do not use general fabric guidance as proof that a specific product is currently in stock.
11. For current products, pricing, stock, reviews or variants, use the current Nari18 product/catalog information.
12. For care instructions, prioritize the care instructions of the individual product when available.
13. When recommending a fabric, consider occasion, season, desired appearance, comfort, maintenance and intended silhouette together.
14. If a customer asks which fabric is "best," first identify what they are buying it for rather than declaring one universal best fabric.
15. Custom stitching availability should be verified at the product level; fabric category alone should not be treated as proof that a specific product supports every customization option.
