# Nari18 Muslin Overview

## 1. Introduction

Muslin is one of the fabric categories offered by Nari18 as part of its premium unstitched ethnic-wear collection. Nari18 positions Muslin as a **soft, lightweight, airy, comfortable fabric** that works particularly well for warm weather, everyday ethnic styling, brunches, intimate gatherings, and relaxed festive occasions.

Nari18's current catalog shows **12 products under the MUSLINS fabric filter**. This count is dynamic and may change as products are added, removed, sold out, or recategorized.

Nari18's fabric guide describes Muslin as having an exceptionally soft texture and airy weave. The fabric drapes delicately and feels lightweight against the skin, making it particularly suitable for hot and humid weather.


## 2. What Is Muslin?

Muslin is a lightweight woven fabric known for its softness, breathability, and fluid drape.

For Nari18's product range, Muslin is positioned differently from more structured fabrics such as Linen and more formal fabrics such as Silk or Tissue.

A useful way to understand the Nari18 Muslin category is:

- **Soft:** comfortable against the skin
- **Lightweight:** suitable for warm weather
- **Airy:** allows a lighter-feeling outfit
- **Fluid:** falls naturally rather than creating a rigid silhouette
- **Matte or understated:** generally less glossy than Silk or Tissue
- **Versatile:** suitable for daily and semi-festive ethnic styling

Nari18 specifically describes contemporary Muslin suits as exceptionally comfortable and naturally elegant, particularly when compared with more structured fabrics.

## 11. Muslin and Chikankari

Muslin has an important historical connection with Chikankari embroidery.

Nari18's Chikankari guide identifies Muslin, particularly fine Mul cotton, as a traditional base for Chikankari. Its lightness and relative transparency historically helped create the characteristic shadow-work effect associated with traditional Nawabi Chikankari.

Nari18 states that its Chikankari range includes Chikankari in:

- Georgette
- Muslin
- Cotton

Therefore, Muslin can be particularly relevant when a customer asks for a traditional or delicate Chikankari-style suit.

### Important RAG distinction

Do not assume that **every Nari18 Muslin suit is a Chikankari suit**.

Muslin is the fabric category. Chikankari is an embroidery technique. A specific Muslin product must explicitly mention Chikankari before the product should be described as Chikankari.

## 14. Lining and Opacity

Because Muslin can be lightweight and fine, opacity should be considered when planning the final stitched garment.

Nari18's summer guide specifically recommends pairing Muslin kurtas with suitable cotton slips or linings where additional opacity is required.

This is especially relevant when:

- The Muslin is light in colour
- The weave is particularly fine
- The kurta is intended for daytime wear
- The customer prefers greater coverage
- The design requires a clean finished silhouette

Do not assume that every Muslin product requires lining. The requirement depends on the exact fabric, colour, weave, construction, and desired finished garment.

## 18. Muslin Care — High-Level Guidance

Nari18's fabric guide lists **gentle hand wash** as the general care recommendation for Muslin.

Its summer guide provides more detailed guidance:

- Hand wash or use a delicate cycle
- Do not wring
- Roll in a towel to remove excess water


For Chikankari Muslin, Nari18 recommends turning the garment inside out, using cold water and mild detergent, avoiding wringing, drying in shade, and using reverse-side ironing with a cloth between the iron and fabric. Heavily embellished pieces may require dry cleaning.

The dedicated `muslin_care.md` file should be used for detailed care questions.
