# Nari18 Custom Tailoring

## Overview

Nari18 offers custom tailoring for unstitched suit sets through its in-house tailoring team. The tailoring service converts the selected fabric into a finished garment according to the customer's measurements, preferred silhouette, neckline, sleeve style, and other agreed styling requirements.

Nari18 positions its tailoring service as a boutique-style, made-to-measure option rather than standard-size ready-to-wear stitching. The website states that customers can share their measurements and styling preferences with the Nari18 team after ordering, with the finished garment prepared by the in-house tailoring team.

## What Custom Tailoring Means at Nari18

A Nari18 unstitched suit is supplied as fabric rather than as a finished garment. Custom tailoring is the process of cutting and constructing that fabric into the customer's desired outfit.

Tailoring can involve:

- Taking and applying customer measurements
- Selecting the garment silhouette
- Determining kurta length
- Selecting neckline style
- Selecting sleeve style and sleeve length
- Determining bottom length and construction
- Considering lining requirements
- Planning embroidery, border, or panel placement
- Applying appropriate seam and finishing techniques
- Adjusting construction according to the behaviour of the fabric

The final construction should be based on the actual product, fabric, available material, and the customer's confirmed preferences.

## Custom Tailoring Process

### 1. Select the Unstitched Suit

The customer first selects an unstitched Nari18 suit set.

Before tailoring, the product information should be reviewed for:

- Fabric name
- Fibre or fabric composition where provided
- Kurta fabric
- Bottom fabric
- Dupatta fabric
- Embroidery or other surface work
- Borders and decorative panels
- Fabric dimensions
- Lining information
- Care instructions
- Product-specific stitching availability

Nari18's fabric-care guidance specifically recommends reviewing the product information before cutting because different pieces in the same set may use different fabrics or require different care.

### 2. Confirm Tailoring Availability

Nari18's general website promotes custom stitching for its unstitched collection. However, individual collection pages use wording such as "where stitching is offered on a product" and direct customers to checkout or the Nari18 team for bespoke questions.

Therefore, the **current product page and checkout should be treated as the final reference for a specific product's tailoring availability**.

### 3. Share Measurements

The customer provides the measurements needed to construct the garment.

Nari18's published tailoring guidance identifies the following measurements:

| Measurement | Tailoring purpose |
| Bust | Determines upper-body garment fit |
| Waist | Determines waist fit |
| Hip | Helps determine lower kurta fit |
| Shoulder width | Controls shoulder and sleeve placement |
| Kurta length | Determines finished kurta length |
| Sleeve length | Determines sleeve size and proportion |
| Bottom inseam | Helps determine bottom length |

Nari18 recommends taking measurements while wearing the intended undergarment and avoiding measurement over thick clothing. For online custom orders, Nari18 states that it provides a detailed measurement guide.

## Measurement Accuracy

Custom tailoring depends heavily on the accuracy of the measurements provided.

Customers should:

- Use a proper measuring tape.
- Keep the tape comfortably fitted rather than excessively tight.
- Take measurements over the undergarment intended for the outfit.
- Avoid measuring over jackets, sweaters, or other bulky clothing.
- Keep the measuring posture natural.
- Recheck measurements before submitting them.
- Clearly communicate any fit preference to the tailoring team.

The measurements supplied to Nari18 should represent the customer's intended finished fit rather than simply copying the size of another ready-made garment.

## Choosing the Silhouette

The silhouette determines the overall shape and fall of the finished suit.

Nari18's tailoring guidance discusses several options:

### Straight Cut

A straight-cut kurta follows a relatively straight vertical shape. It can be paired with straight-leg bottoms or other suitable bottom styles.

### A-Line

An A-line kurta gradually widens toward the hem. It provides more flare than a straight-cut silhouette while remaining relatively structured.

### Flared / Anarkali Style

A flared Anarkali-style silhouette creates greater volume and movement through the lower portion of the garment.

### Palazzo Style

The suit can be constructed with a palazzo-style bottom where the selected fabric and available material support that design.

### Sharara Style

A sharara-style bottom can be considered where sufficient fabric and the particular suit design make it appropriate.

### Short Kurta

Some fabrics and suit designs can be adapted into shorter kurta silhouettes where appropriate.

Nari18 advises customers to discuss silhouette choices with its design team when ordering custom stitching.

## Neckline Customisation

The neckline has a major effect on the appearance of the finished kurta.

Nari18's published custom-tailoring guidance identifies examples such as:

- Round neck
- V-neck
- Boat neck
- Keyhole neckline
- Mandarin collar

The selected neckline should take into account the existing embroidery, neckline panel, border, print placement, and available fabric.

A neckline should not be selected purely from a generic style list if the product contains a fixed embroidered or decorative neckline panel. The actual fabric design should be checked before cutting.

## Sleeve Customisation

Common sleeve options discussed by Nari18 include:

- Full sleeves
- Three-quarter sleeves
- Half sleeves
- Sleeveless

The sleeve length and construction should be confirmed before cutting because the available fabric and existing decorative work can affect the feasible sleeve design.

## Fabric-Specific Tailoring

Different fabrics behave differently during cutting, stitching, pressing, and finishing. Nari18 specifically highlights the importance of using a tailor familiar with premium ethnic fabrics.

### Silk

Silk can have a smooth surface and a rich drape. Stitching should account for the fabric's weight, surface finish, and any embroidery or decorative work.

### Chanderi

Chanderi can have a lighter and more delicate structure. Seam finishing and handling should be appropriate to the fabric.

### Organza

Organza can be lightweight, structured, and translucent. Lining and seam finishing may therefore become especially important.

### Tissue

Tissue fabrics can have a structured or shimmering surface and may contain decorative work. Cutting and pressing should protect the surface and embellishment.

### Georgette

Georgette generally has a fluid drape. The finished silhouette and seam construction should account for this movement.

### Cotton

Cotton can be suitable for more practical everyday silhouettes. However, exact construction still depends on the particular cotton fabric, print, embroidery, and care requirements.

### Linen

Linen has a more structured natural appearance. Garment design should account for its characteristic texture and fall.

### Muslin

Muslin is lightweight and soft with a fluid drape. The final construction may benefit from appropriate lining or support depending on the fabric's opacity and intended design.

Nari18's guides repeatedly emphasise that premium fabrics such as Silk, Chanderi, Organza, and other delicate materials should be handled by a tailor familiar with their characteristics.

## Lining Considerations

Lining can be an important part of custom tailoring.

Before cutting, the customer and tailoring team may need to determine:

- Whether lining is required
- Which lining material is appropriate
- Whether the fabric is transparent or semi-transparent
- Whether embroidery requires additional support
- How the lining should be attached
- Whether the lining should follow the entire garment or selected sections

Nari18's current website specifically mentions that customers can contact the team for guidance regarding measurements, lining, and finishing.

For fabrics such as Organza or other sheer materials, lining requirements should be confirmed before cutting.

## Embroidery and Decorative Placement

Unstitched suit sets can contain:

- Embroidered panels
- Neckline work
- Borders
- Bootis
- Zari work
- Thread embroidery
- Decorative motifs
- Printed patterns
- Embellishments

These elements can influence where the fabric should be cut.

Nari18's fabric-care guidance recommends photographing the complete fabric set and close views of important design elements before cutting. This creates a reference for the tailor and helps preserve the intended placement of the design.


## Pre-Stitching Inspection

Before custom tailoring begins, the fabric should be checked.

Recommended checks include:

1. Confirm that all expected pieces are present.
2. Review the product description.
3. Check the fabric composition where provided.
4. Identify the kurta, bottom, and dupatta pieces.
5. Check embroidery and border placement.
6. Check the fabric length and width.
7. Identify any lining requirements.
8. Review care instructions.
9. Photograph important decorative areas.
10. Ask Nari18 about any unclear or unusual feature before cutting.

Nari18 specifically recommends confirming uncertain fibre, dye, finish, and cleaning information before performing irreversible work such as cutting.

## Tailoring and Fabric Preparation

Fabric preparation should be determined by the specific material.

Customers should not automatically pre-wash every unstitched suit before tailoring.

The appropriate approach depends on:

- Fibre composition
- Dye
- Finish
- Embroidery
- Fabric stability
- Expected shrinkage
- Manufacturer or product care instructions

Nari18 recommends confirming whether any controlled pre-washing is appropriate for the exact material before cutting rather than applying a generic washing method to every fabric.

## Finishing Considerations

Custom tailoring is not limited to basic stitching. Finishing affects the final appearance and durability of the garment.

Relevant finishing areas can include:

- Neckline finishing
- Sleeve finishing
- Hem finishing
- Seam finishing
- Lining attachment
- Dupatta edging
- Bottom construction
- Placement of decorative borders
- Overall garment fall

The appropriate finishing method depends on the fabric and product design.

For embellished fabrics, excessive heat or pressure during pressing can damage decorative elements. Nari18's care guidance recommends following the product-specific instructions and using appropriate protection when pressing decorated material.

## Custom Tailoring vs Standard Stitching

| Feature | Custom Tailoring | Standard Ready-to-Wear |
| Fit | Based on supplied measurements | Based on standard size |
| Silhouette | Customer preference can be discussed | Predetermined |
| Neckline | Can be selected from available options | Predetermined |
| Sleeve style | Can be discussed | Predetermined |
| Kurta length | Based on customer measurements/preferences | Predetermined |
| Fabric | Customer selects an unstitched suit | Already constructed |
| Design flexibility | Higher | Lower |
| Tailoring involvement | Required | Already completed |

Custom tailoring is therefore particularly useful for customers who want a personalised fit or specific styling rather than a standard-size garment.

## Important Limitations

Custom tailoring should not be interpreted as unlimited garment modification.

Do not assume that Nari18 will:

- Change the fundamental design of every product
- Add embroidery that is not supplied
- Supply additional fabric
- Guarantee every requested neckline or silhouette
- Modify every decorative panel
- Provide unlimited alterations
- Stitch every product without product-level confirmation

The feasible tailoring options depend on the particular suit, available fabric, design, and the requirements agreed with Nari18.

## Custom-Tailored Order Policy

Customers should confirm measurements and styling requirements carefully before tailoring.

Nari18's current website states that custom-stitched orders are not exchangeable, while manufacturing-defect exchanges have a separate 7-day provision. The exact current policy and applicable conditions should be checked against Nari18's current terms and promise before answering a policy-sensitive customer question.

This distinction is important:

**Fit preference or customer-supplied measurement issue ≠ manufacturing defect.**

Policy questions should therefore be answered from the current Nari18 policy rather than from general tailoring guidance.

## RAG Retrieval Rules

When answering questions about Nari18 custom tailoring:

1. Treat custom tailoring as the conversion of an unstitched suit into a made-to-measure finished garment.
2. Use the current product page or checkout to confirm whether tailoring is offered for a particular product.
3. Do not automatically claim that every product has every possible tailoring option.
4. Use Nari18's published measurements when answering measurement questions.
5. Core measurements include bust, waist, hip, shoulder width, kurta length, sleeve length, and bottom inseam.
6. Do not invent a customer's measurements.
7. Do not infer a lining requirement without considering the actual fabric and product.
8. Check decorative panels, embroidery, borders, and fabric dimensions before discussing cutting.
9. Do not recommend generic pre-washing for every fabric.
10. Use product-specific care instructions when available.
11. Treat fabric-specific tailoring as important for Silk, Chanderi, Organza, Tissue, Georgette, Muslin, Linen, and other delicate or structured materials.
12. Do not promise unlimited customisation.
13. Do not claim additional embroidery, fabric, or design changes unless explicitly confirmed.
14. For exchange questions involving custom-tailored garments, use the current Nari18 policy.
15. For current tailoring availability, prioritise the current product/checkout information over older blog wording.
