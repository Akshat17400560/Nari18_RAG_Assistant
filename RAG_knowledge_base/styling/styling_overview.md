# Nari18 Styling Overview

## Overview

Nari18 approaches styling as more than simply selecting a suit. The overall look is built by considering the **fabric, occasion, colour, silhouette, embroidery or surface detailing, dupatta, bottom, accessories, and desired level of formality**.

Nari18's current website describes the brand as a premium destination for unstitched suits and customized tailoring, with styling support available online and at its Gurugram boutique. The brand's styling content particularly emphasizes matching fabrics and silhouettes to the occasion rather than treating every suit as interchangeable.

The Nari18 homepage also presents style reels showing the movement, drape, embroidery, and silhouette of its suits, indicating that the visual behaviour of a garment is an important part of the styling decision.

## Nari18 Styling Philosophy

A Nari18 styling recommendation should generally consider five connected decisions:

1. **What is the occasion?**
2. **Which fabric suits the occasion and season?**
3. **Which silhouette complements the fabric and desired look?**
4. **How should colour, embroidery, and the dupatta be balanced?**
5. **Which accessories and finishing details complete the outfit?**

The objective is to create a coordinated outfit rather than adding as many decorative elements as possible.

Nari18's current styling guide provides a simple occasion-based fabric map:

- **Silk and Tissue:** weddings, bridal events, grand celebrations
- **Chanderi and Georgette:** festive occasions and wedding-guest dressing
- **Cotton, Linen and Muslin:** everyday and office wear
- **Organza and Chiffon:** evening and party wear

This is a general styling framework. Individual product details should always take priority.

## Styling by Occasion

### Wedding and Bridal Events

For weddings and bridal-related events, Nari18's guides position **Silk and Tissue** among the strongest choices.

These fabrics can create a richer and more formal appearance, particularly when combined with:

- Elaborate embroidery
- Zari or metallic detailing where present
- Rich dupattas
- Structured or elegant silhouettes
- Statement jewellery
- Dressier footwear

A heavily detailed suit does not necessarily require equally heavy accessories. When the garment already contains substantial embroidery or surface work, jewellery and other accessories can be kept more controlled.

### Festive Occasions

For festivals and traditional celebrations, **Chanderi and Georgette** are highlighted by Nari18 as suitable options, while Silk and Tissue can be chosen when a more luxurious appearance is desired.

Festive styling can combine:

- Traditional silhouettes
- Decorative dupattas
- Jewellery with ethnic character
- Embroidered or woven details
- Rich or jewel-toned colours
- Coordinated footwear

The final level of decoration should depend on the product's existing design.

### Evening and Party Wear

Nari18's current guide associates **Organza and Chiffon** with evening and party wear.

These fabrics can work particularly well when the styling objective is:

- Elegant rather than heavily traditional
- Lightweight but visually distinctive
- Graceful movement
- Refined evening dressing
- A contemporary ethnic appearance

Organza can provide more structure, while fluid fabrics such as Chiffon can create softer movement. Exact behaviour depends on the individual fabric and product.

### Office and Everyday Wear

Nari18 recommends **Cotton, Linen and Muslin** among the more practical choices for everyday and office styling.

A practical everyday look can emphasize:

- Comfortable silhouettes
- Breathable fabrics
- Minimal accessories
- Simple footwear
- Subtle prints or embroidery
- Easy-to-maintain styling

For office wear, the overall appearance can remain polished without relying on heavy embellishment.

### Summer Styling

Nari18's summer guide identifies Cotton, Linen and Muslin as key warm-weather fabrics, with Chanderi also positioned as a useful festive-summer choice.

For summer styling, prioritize:

- Breathable fabrics
- Comfortable ease
- Lightweight dupattas
- Less restrictive silhouettes
- Minimal layering
- Comfortable footwear

Muslin is particularly suited to soft, airy styling, while Linen can create a more structured appearance.

## Styling by Fabric

### Silk

Silk creates a naturally rich and festive impression.

Best suited to:

- Weddings
- Sangeet and celebrations
- Festive occasions
- Formal ethnic events
- Bridal trousseau styling

Silk can be styled with either traditional or more streamlined silhouettes. Accessories should be chosen according to the amount of existing embroidery, zari, motifs, or other surface work.

Nari18's current Silk collection describes its suits as suitable for weddings, celebrations, festive occasions, and refined everyday styling.

### Tissue

Tissue and Tissue Silk can provide a shimmering, celebratory appearance.

Suitable styling directions include:

- Weddings
- Poojas
- Festive celebrations
- Evening occasions
- Traditional functions

Because Tissue can already provide visual shine, styling does not always require excessive additional sparkle.

### Organza

Organza is associated with lightweight, graceful and elegant styling.

It can work well for:

- Receptions
- Evening events
- Parties
- Weddings
- Formal gatherings

When styling Organza, consider transparency, structure, embroidery, and the visual balance between the kurta and dupatta.

### Chanderi

Chanderi can bridge everyday comfort and festive elegance.

It can be styled for:

- Festive daytime events
- Family gatherings
- Cultural occasions
- Refined summer dressing
- Semi-formal occasions

Its styling can range from understated to more traditional depending on embroidery, border work, colour, and accessories.

### Georgette

Georgette is useful when the desired look involves movement and a softer drape.

It can suit:

- Festive occasions
- Wedding guests
- Evening events
- Chikankari styling
- Flowing silhouettes

Nari18 also features Chikankari work in Georgette, Muslin, and Cotton suit sets.

### Cotton

Cotton is a practical foundation for everyday and summer styling.

It can be dressed up or down through:

- Jewellery
- Dupatta selection
- Footwear
- Silhouette
- Colour combinations
- Embroidery or print

### Linen

Linen can create a clean, relaxed and sophisticated appearance.

It is particularly suitable for:

- Daywear
- Office dressing
- Summer events
- Minimal ethnic styling

A structured silhouette can complement Linen's natural character.

### Muslin

Muslin works well for soft, lightweight and comfortable styling.

It is suitable for:

- Everyday wear
- Warm weather
- Brunches
- Intimate gatherings
- Relaxed ethnic looks

Nari18's summer guidance specifically positions Muslin for brunches, intimate gatherings and elevated everyday wear.

## Styling by Silhouette

The silhouette should complement the fabric rather than being selected independently.

### Straight-Cut

A straight-cut suit creates a clean and versatile appearance.

Suitable for:

- Office wear
- Everyday dressing
- Festive styling
- Formal occasions

It can be especially useful when the fabric or embroidery is already visually strong.

### A-Line

An A-line silhouette creates gentle flare while maintaining a relatively clean structure.

It can work across:

- Festive occasions
- Family gatherings
- Day events
- Semi-formal dressing

### Flared Anarkali

A flared Anarkali creates a more dramatic and traditional appearance.

It is particularly suitable when:

- The fabric has enough fall and volume
- The occasion is festive or formal
- A statement silhouette is desired

The amount of flare should be decided according to fabric quantity and product construction.

### Palazzo Styling

Palazzo bottoms can create a contemporary ethnic appearance.

They can work particularly well with:

- Shorter kurtas
- Contemporary styling
- Lightweight fabrics
- Casual or semi-formal looks

### Sharara Styling

Sharara styling provides a more traditional festive character.

It is particularly appropriate for:

- Weddings
- Festive celebrations
- Mehendi-style events
- Statement ethnic looks

The final feasibility depends on the available fabric and product construction.

## Colour Styling

Colour should be considered together with the fabric and level of detailing.

### Soft and Neutral Colours

Ivory, cream, beige, blush, dusty pink and similar shades can create an understated and elegant appearance.

These colours can be particularly useful when:

- The fabric itself has a premium texture
- Embroidery is delicate
- Minimal styling is desired
- The occasion is daytime or refined formal

### Jewel and Deep Colours

Deep blue, magenta, red, emerald-like green and other rich shades can create a stronger festive appearance.

They can be paired with:

- Traditional jewellery
- Metallic accents
- Rich dupattas
- Festive footwear

### Pastels

Pastel colours can create a lighter and more contemporary ethnic look.

They can be particularly effective for:

- Daytime celebrations
- Summer occasions
- Brunches
- Elegant minimal styling

### Metallic and Shimmering Tones

Gold, copper, antique-gold and similar finishes can create a more celebratory appearance.

When the garment already has metallic detailing, additional metallic accessories should be balanced carefully to avoid making the outfit visually heavy.

## Dupatta Styling

The dupatta can significantly change the final appearance of an unstitched suit.

Consider:

- Dupatta fabric
- Border width
- Embroidery
- Print
- Transparency
- Colour contrast
- Drape
- Occasion

A heavily embroidered dupatta can function as the main statement element, allowing the kurta and jewellery to remain comparatively simple.

A simpler dupatta can allow the neckline, embroidery, jewellery or silhouette to become the focal point.

For delicate fabrics such as Organza, the drape and border should be considered carefully because the dupatta can strongly influence the visual balance of the complete outfit.

## Jewellery Styling

Jewellery should complement the garment rather than compete with it.

### Heavily Embroidered Suit

Consider:

- One strong necklace or earrings
- Controlled bangles
- Minimal additional embellishment

### Minimal Suit

A simpler suit can accommodate:

- Statement earrings
- Layered necklaces
- Traditional jewellery
- Decorative bangles

### Festive Silk or Tissue

Traditional or metallic jewellery can reinforce the richness of the fabric.

### Cotton, Linen or Muslin

Light jewellery, silver-toned pieces, small earrings, or understated traditional accessories can maintain the relaxed character of the outfit.

These are styling recommendations rather than fixed Nari18 product rules.

## Footwear Styling

Footwear should match the formality and silhouette of the outfit.

Possible directions include:

- Juttis for traditional ethnic styling
- Sandals for relaxed daytime looks
- Heels for formal or evening occasions
- Minimal footwear for lightweight everyday suits

The colour can either coordinate with the outfit or provide a controlled contrast.

## Balancing Embroidery and Accessories

One of the most important styling principles is visual balance.

If the suit contains:

- Heavy neckline embroidery → keep the necklace simpler
- Elaborate dupatta → reduce other statement elements
- Strong zari work → avoid excessive competing metallic accessories
- Minimal embroidery → jewellery can become the focal point
- Strong print → use simpler accessories
- Statement silhouette → avoid unnecessarily complicated layering

The objective is to establish one or two focal points rather than making every element equally prominent.

## Styling for Day vs Evening

### Daytime

Daytime styling generally benefits from:

- Lighter fabrics
- Softer colours
- Comfortable silhouettes
- Minimal jewellery
- Natural or understated finishing
- Breathable construction

Cotton, Linen, Muslin and lighter Chanderi options are useful starting points depending on the occasion.

### Evening

Evening styling can accommodate:

- Richer colours
- Silk
- Tissue
- Organza
- Chiffon
- More elaborate embroidery
- Statement jewellery
- Dressier footwear

Nari18's current guide specifically places Organza and Chiffon in evening/party styling and Silk/Tissue in more formal celebratory categories.

## Styling the Same Suit for Different Occasions

An unstitched suit can potentially be styled differently depending on how it is stitched and accessorized.

For example:

**Daytime**
- Simpler jewellery
- Comfortable footwear
- Lighter styling
- Less dramatic makeup

**Festive**
- Traditional jewellery
- More decorative footwear
- Richer styling
- More structured drape

**Evening**
- Statement earrings
- Dressier footwear
- More polished finishing
- Stronger accessories

The same fabric does not automatically work for every occasion. Product detailing, colour, embroidery, and silhouette must also be considered.

## Styling Decision Framework

For RAG-based recommendations, use this sequence:

### Step 1 — Identify the occasion

Determine whether the customer is dressing for:

- Wedding
- Bridal event
- Festive occasion
- Evening party
- Office
- Everyday wear
- Summer daytime event
- Family gathering
- Brunch
- Formal event

### Step 2 — Identify the desired mood

Examples:

- Elegant
- Minimal
- Traditional
- Festive
- Luxurious
- Contemporary
- Comfortable
- Statement-making

### Step 3 — Select the fabric family

Use the Nari18 occasion/fabric guidance as the starting point.

### Step 4 — Evaluate the product details

Check:

- Colour
- Embroidery
- Print
- Zari
- Border
- Dupatta
- Bottom
- Fabric composition
- Current product description

### Step 5 — Choose the silhouette

Match the silhouette to:

- Fabric drape
- Fabric quantity
- Existing design
- Occasion
- Desired styling direction

### Step 6 — Balance accessories

Choose jewellery, footwear and other accessories according to the visual weight of the outfit.

### Step 7 — Consider the final finish

The final appearance depends on:

- Fit
- Drape
- Dupatta placement
- Footwear
- Jewellery
- Hair and makeup
- Occasion
